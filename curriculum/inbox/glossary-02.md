- **round brush** — a brush whose hairs come to one sharp point; pressing spreads the hairs for a wide line, the tip alone paints a thin line (02.1)
- **loaded to the tip** — paint on the front half of the brush hairs only, with the hairs shaped into a sharp point and no drips (02.1)
- **pressure stroke** — a brush stroke that goes thin, thick, thin: touch with the tip, press while pulling, then lift (02.1)
- **belly (of a brush)** — the fat middle part of the hairs of a round brush, which spreads out when you press (02.1)
- **teardrop** — a stroke with a round head and a sharp tail: press for the head, then lift while pulling away (02.2)
- **petal** — a teardrop or flat-brush stroke with the wide end on the outside of a flower and the thin end pointing to the center (02.2)
- **flat brush** — a brush with a straight, flat edge; pressing the whole edge gives a wide mark (02.2)
- **filbert** — a flat brush with a rounded edge, good for soft petals and leaves (02.2)
- **curl** — a pressure stroke that keeps turning and ends in a thin point at its center (02.3)
- **spiral** — a line that winds around and around a center point, getting smaller each turn (02.3)
- **S swirl** — two curls joined in the middle and turning opposite ways, like the letter S (02.3)
- **dotting tool** — a small stick with a round metal ball on each end, used to paint round dots (02.4)
- **dot trail** — a line of dots that get smaller, painted from a single dip of the tool (02.4)
- **dot flower** — a flower made only of dots: a ring of dots around a center dot (02.4)
- **stroke sampler** — a small practice piece that shows one row of each stroke you can paint (02.5)
- **contrast** — a strong difference between two colors, such as light paint on dark skin, so the paint is easy to see (02.5)
