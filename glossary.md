# Glossary

Words used in this course, in plain language. The lesson in brackets is where the word first comes up.

- **60-30-10** — a guide for sharing colors: about 60% main color, 30% second color and 10% accent (03.5)
- **accent** — a small touch of a different color that catches the eye (03.5)
- **activate** — wetting a paint cake so its top layer turns into creamy, usable paint (01.4)
- **allergic reaction** — redness, itching, swelling or a rash because the body reacts to one ingredient (01.3)
- **analogous colors** — colors that are neighbors on the color wheel, like blue, purple and pink (03.5)
- **anchor dots** — tiny light dots that mark the corners and ends of lines before you paint them (07.2)
- **base** — a layer of sponged color that covers a large area before the details are painted on top (04.5)
- **beading** — water-based paint pulling into little drops on oily skin or wet cream instead of a smooth layer (05.2)
- **belly (of a brush)** — the fat middle part of the hairs of a round brush, which spreads out when you press (02.1)
- **bio glitter (plant-cellulose glitter)** — glitter made from a film of plant cellulose instead of plastic; lower-impact, but not harmless to nature (06.1)
- **black henna** — a dark "henna" sold for temporary tattoos that often contains PPD and can cause blisters, scars and lifelong allergy; never use it (01.2)
- **black light (UV torch)** — a lamp that gives off ultraviolet light (often 365 nm) and makes fluorescent colors glow; never shone into anyone's eyes (06.4)
- **blending zone** — the strip where two colors in a gradient overlap and mix (08.1)
- **brief** — a short note of where a look will be worn, for how long, and in what conditions (07.4)
- **brow bone** — the slightly raised bone just under the eyebrow (05.4)
- **C-scroll** — one line that curls into a tight spiral at one end (09.2)
- **C-stroke** — a curved pressure stroke shaped like the letter C; turned on its side it becomes the U of a freehand scale (08.3)
- **cake** — a solid pot or pan of face paint (01.1)
- **cast shadow** — the soft dark shape a painted object seems to throw onto the skin beside it, on the side away from the light (08.2)
- **cellulose** — the material plant walls are made of; used to make plant-based glitter flakes (06.1)
- **center line** — the line down the middle of the face, through the forehead, nose, lips and chin (03.4)
- **chevron** — a V shape; stacked chevrons point in one direction, for example up and out on the cheekbone (07.2)
- **CI number** — Colour Index number, a code such as CI 77891 that names one color ingredient on a cosmetic label (01.2)
- **closed-eye design** — an eye design painted on the moving lid, seen mainly when the eyes are closed (05.4)
- **cluster** — a small group of shapes (for example one big and two small flowers) painted together as one design (04.1)
- **color band** — a strip of color between two nested lines (07.3)
- **color blocking** — painting flat areas of solid color side by side, each with a clean edge, instead of blending them (05.1)
- **color story** — a palette plus the mood it should give, written as three mood words (09.3)
- **color wheel** — a circle of colors in order (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple), used to find colors that mix and match well (03.1)
- **complementary colors** — colors opposite each other on the color wheel, like blue and orange (03.5)
- **complementary pop** — a small touch of the opposite color next to a big area of the main color, which makes both look stronger (05.1)
- **consistency** — how thick or runny the paint is; "just right" is creamy, like melted ice cream (01.4)
- **constraint** — a limit you choose on purpose, such as three colors or one side only, to make design choices easier (09.4)
- **contrast** — a strong difference between two colors, such as light paint on dark skin, so the paint is easy to see (02.5)
- **CosIng** — the European Commission's free online database of cosmetic ingredients, where you can look up a CI number (01.2)
- **cosmetic** — a product made and sold to be put on the body, such as face paint or makeup, which must follow cosmetic safety rules (01.1)
- **cosmetic glitter** — glitter sold for face and body, with an ingredient list; the only glitter that goes on skin (06.1)
- **craft glitter** — glitter sold for cards, slime and decorations; not made or checked as a cosmetic, often with bigger, sharp-edged flakes; never on skin (06.1)
- **crease** — the fold of the upper eyelid that appears when the eye is open (05.4)
- **curl** — a pressure stroke that keeps turning and ends in a thin point at its center (02.3)
- **dab** — pressing a sponge onto the skin and lifting it straight off, instead of wiping (01.4)
- **dot drag** — a dot with a tail pulled out of it by the tool or brush tip; used as a petal in dotwork (07.1)
- **dot flower** — a flower made only of dots: a ring of dots around a center dot (02.4)
- **dot trail** — a line of dots that get smaller, painted from a single dip of the tool (02.4)
- **dotting tool** — a small stick with a round metal ball on each end, used to paint round dots (02.4)
- **dotwork** — designs built mostly from dots of different sizes placed in rings, arcs and trails (07.1)
- **echo line** — one thin line that follows the outside of a design at a small, even distance (07.3)
- **emblem** — a small symbol, such as a star or a lightning bolt, painted on the forehead of a hero design (04.6)
- **eye almond** — the wide almond of bare skin left around each eye inside a painted mask (09.2)
- **eye area** — the brows, eyelids and the skin just under the eyes; only products whose label allows it may go there (01.2)
- **face gems** — small flat-backed stones made for skin, often self-adhesive, used as jewels in a design (04.6)
- **face map** — a plan of the face with a center line, cross lines and landmarks, used to place designs (03.4)
- **facet** — one flat side of a cut gem; each facet gets one value, lighter if it faces the light (08.2)
- **fade into the skin** — dabbing more lightly and with less paint at the edges, so color ends softly with no hard border (03.2)
- **ferrule** — the metal part of a brush that holds the hairs (01.5)
- **festival face map** — the face map with five festival zones: brow-to-temple arc, cheekbone line, third eye, hairline and chin (05.3)
- **filbert** — a flat brush with a rounded edge, good for soft petals and leaves (02.2)
- **filigree** — fine, curling lines like delicate metalwork, painted with a liner brush (09.2)
- **flat brush** — a brush with a straight, flat edge; pressing the whole edge gives a wide mark (02.2)
- **floating crease** — a line painted just above the natural crease so it shows with the eyes open (05.4)
- **fluorescent (neon, UV-reactive)** — color that turns invisible ultraviolet light into bright visible color, so it glows under a black light; never near the eyes (06.4)
- **fluorescent (neon)** — very bright colors that glow under a UV black light; none are allowed near the eyes (01.2)
- **glitter base** — a thin, slightly sticky skin-safe layer (cosmetic glitter gel, aloe vera gel, or a little petroleum jelly on the body) that glitter is patted onto so it stays put (06.2)
- **glitter roots** — glitter along the parting of the hair and the hairline (06.2)
- **glow-in-the-dark** — paint that shines in the dark after light has charged it; only for occasional use and never near the eyes (01.2)
- **graded line** — a line of gems or dots that gets smaller step by step from one end to the other (06.3)
- **gradient** — a soft change from one color to another, with no line where they meet (03.2)
- **graphic wing** — eyeliner along the upper lash line that flicks out to a sharp point (05.4)
- **highlight** — a small white mark on the side of a shape where the light hits it (03.3)
- **hygiene** — keeping hands, tools and the work space clean so germs don't pass from one person to the next (01.3)
- **illusion** — a design that tricks the eye, such as a crack, a zipper or peeled skin painted on flat skin (08.4)
- **ingredient list** — the list of everything a cosmetic is made of, on the pack or the seller's page; colors appear in it as CI numbers or color names (01.2)
- **irritation** — redness or stinging because a product is too harsh for the skin, even without an allergy (01.3)
- **iterate** — improve a design in versions, changing one thing each time after a self-check (09.4)
- **key features** — the 3-5 things everyone connects with a theme, such as waves, shells and pearls for "ocean"; a design keeps only these (09.1)
- **kohl** — a traditional eyeliner (also kajal or surma) that often contains lead; avoid it (01.2)
- **label** — the printed text on a product or its pack, including the product type, the ingredient list and any warnings (01.2)
- **landmark** — a point you can find on every face, such as the end of the brow or the outer eye corner, used to measure where things go (03.4)
- **lash line** — the edge of the eyelid where the lashes grow; paint, glitter and gems stay off it (04.3)
- **lead line** — the first, most important flowing line of a wave design; the other lines copy it (07.3)
- **light direction** — the side light comes from in a design; all highlights go on that side (03.3)
- **linework** — thin black (or white) lines between and around color areas that keep each color clear (05.1)
- **load** — picking up paint with a brush or sponge (01.4)
- **loaded to the tip** — paint on the front half of the brush hairs only, with the hairs shaped into a sharp point and no drips (02.1)
- **main texture** — the one surface pattern that makes a character believable, such as scales, wing veins or leaves; it gets the most space (09.3)
- **mandala** — a round design that grows from one center in even rings, the same in every direction (07.1)
- **masquerade mask** — a painted mask around the eyes, usually rising to points at the temples and decorated with lace, filigree and gems; the eyes stay bare (09.2)
- **micellar water** — a gentle cleansing water used on a cotton pad to lift makeup and strong face paint colors (01.5)
- **microplastics** — tiny pieces of plastic, such as plastic glitter flakes; the EU restricts adding them to products on purpose (Regulation 2023/2055) (06.1)
- **midtone** — the plain color of a shape, where the light falls evenly, between the highlight and the shadow (08.2)
- **monochrome** — one color used in light, middle and dark versions (03.5)
- **muddy** — a dull, brownish or grayish mix, usually from mixing three or more colors, opposite colors, or dirty water (03.1)
- **muzzle** — the white puffs on the upper lip of an animal face, where the whiskers grow (04.5)
- **neon look** — bright colors placed next to black and white so they seem to glow, without UV paint (05.1)
- **nesting** — painting lines beside each other at the same gap, each copying the line next to it (07.3)
- **non-toxic** — a label on art materials meaning safe for art use as intended; it does not mean safe for skin (01.2)
- **oil cleanser** — a gentle oil-based face cleanser that dissolves cream or oil-based paint; eye-safe versions can be used around the eyes (01.5)
- **one-second read** — the test of a design: can someone recognize it in one second from about three meters away? (09.1)
- **one-stroke** — painting several colors at once with one brush loaded from a split cake (04.4)
- **open-eye design** — an eye design painted with the eyes open, made to be seen that way (05.4)
- **optical vibration** — the shimmering effect when two strong, opposite colors sit side by side (07.3)
- **order of work** — the fixed sequence for a full look: prep, sketch, color, linework, dots, eye makeup, setting, glitter and gems (07.4)
- **outline** — a thin black line around the outside of a shape that makes its edge clear (03.3)
- **overlap zone** — the middle of a gradient, where two colors are dabbed over each other and blend (03.2)
- **palette** — the small set of colors one design uses (03.5)
- **patch test** — painting a small spot of a new product on the inner forearm and watching it for 24-48 hours before using it on a face (01.3)
- **PET (polyethylene terephthalate)** — a common plastic; a glitter that lists it in its ingredients is plastic glitter (06.1)
- **petal** — a teardrop or flat-brush stroke with the wide end on the outside of a flower and the thin end pointing to the center (02.2)
- **placing by quarters** — putting dots at the top, bottom and both sides first, then halfway between, so a ring stays even (07.1)
- **pop** — when a shape stands out clearly because of strong light-and-dark contrast (03.3)
- **porcelain crack** — a painted crack on a pale base, with a soft shadow on one side of the line and a white shine on the other (08.4)
- **portfolio** — a collection of your best finished designs, each with photos and notes (09.4)
- **PPD** — para-phenylenediamine, a hair dye chemical found in "black henna" that can cause serious skin reactions (01.2)
- **pressure stroke** — a brush stroke that goes thin, thick, thin: touch with the tip, press while pulling, then lift (02.1)
- **primary colors** — red, yellow and blue: the three starting colors that can't be mixed from others (03.1)
- **primer** — an optional thin skin product put on before paint or makeup to help it go on smoothly and last (05.2)
- **prosthetic adhesive** — a strong skin adhesive (for example Pros-Aide) for bigger pieces, away from the eyes (06.3)
- **reference** — a picture you learn one idea from (a color, a shape or a texture), not a design to copy whole (09.4)
- **reflected light** — a faint lighter band just inside the shadow edge of a round shape, where light bounces back (08.2)
- **rim shadow** — a darker shade of the base color just inside the edge of a shape, which makes it look raised (09.2)
- **rotated flower** — a one-stroke flower made by keeping the brush's light corner on the center and turning the brush around it (08.1)
- **round brush** — a brush whose hairs come to one sharp point; pressing spreads the hairs for a wide line, the tip alone paints a thin line (02.1)
- **rub test** — pressing a tissue on a painted, set patch and rubbing it gently to see how well it lasts (05.2)
- **S swirl** — two curls joined in the middle and turning opposite ways, like the letter S (02.3)
- **S-scroll** — two curls joined by one curve, turning in opposite directions (09.2)
- **sacred geometry** — a festival art style of circles, triangles and stars built from evenly spaced points (07.2)
- **saturated color** — the pure, strong version of a color, like hot pink rather than baby pink (05.1)
- **scallop** — one round curve in a row of joined curves, the edge of painted lace (08.3)
- **secondary colors** — orange, green and purple: each one a mix of two primary colors (03.1)
- **self-adhesive face gem** — a flat-backed gem with skin-safe glue already on the back, sold on a sticker sheet (06.3)
- **setting spray** — a spray misted over finished makeup or face paint to help it resist rubbing and sweat (05.2)
- **shade** — a color mixed with a tiny touch of black, so it is darker (03.1)
- **shadow side** — the side of a shape away from the light, where an outline is a little thicker (03.3)
- **simplify** — rebuild a feature from strokes you can already paint (curls, teardrops, dots, lines, U shapes) instead of copying a picture (09.1)
- **sparkle** — a four-point shine made from a long up-down pressure stroke crossed by a shorter side-to-side one (04.2)
- **Special FX (SFX) paint** — paint sold for props, fabric or effects that is not a cosmetic; never on skin (06.4)
- **spiral** — a line that winds around and around a center point, getting smaller each turn (02.3)
- **spirit gum** — a skin adhesive for bigger pieces on the face and body; flammable until dry, needs its own remover, never near the eyes (06.3)
- **split cake** — a face paint cake with several colors side by side, used to load a flat brush with stripes of color for one-stroke designs (04.4)
- **spoke** — an imagined straight line from the center of a mandala outward; rings of dots sit on the spokes or between them (07.1)
- **star zone** — the one main area of a layout with the biggest shape; the other zones are small accents (05.3)
- **stencil** — a thin sheet with holes; paint dabbed through it prints the same sharp shapes every time (08.3)
- **stippling** — applying paint with quick up-and-down dabs of a sponge or brush instead of wiping (03.2)
- **stroke sampler** — a small practice piece that shows one row of each stroke you can paint (02.5)
- **swatch** — a small filled square of one color, painted to see how it looks and behaves (01.4)
- **swatch card** — a sheet with one box per color: a swatch, a line, a dot and a water note (01.4)
- **swoosh** — a curved band of sponged color that gives a small design movement, like the tail of a shooting star (04.2)
- **symmetry** — when the two halves of a design match like mirror images (03.4)
- **taper** — a stroke that gets narrower toward its end, like a tiger stripe going from wide to a point (04.5)
- **teardrop** — a stroke with a round head and a sharp tail: press for the head, then lift while pulling away (02.2)
- **third eye** — a small design on the center line of the forehead, just above the brows (05.3)
- **three-color blend** — a sponge gradient with three colors side by side, so it has two blending zones (08.1)
- **thumbnail** — a tiny, quick sketch of a design idea on a small face outline, made in 3-5 minutes (09.1)
- **tint** — a color mixed with white, so it is lighter and softer, like pink or baby blue (03.1)
- **touch-up kit** — a small bag with what you need to fix a design during the day (05.2)
- **translucent powder** — a colorless face powder pressed over makeup or paint to set it and soak up shine (05.2)
- **upper wings / lower wings** — the parts of a butterfly design above the eyes (over the brows and closed lids) and below them (on the cheeks) (04.3)
- **value** — how light or dark a color is; a value ladder runs from highlight to deep shadow (08.2)
- **vein** — a thin lighter line down the middle of a painted leaf (04.1)
- **vine** — a long, thin stroke, usually green, that ends in a curl and carries leaves or small flowers (04.1)
- **warm and cool colors** — warm: red, orange and yellow (sun, fire); cool: green, blue and purple (ice, water) (03.5)
- **water-activated face paint** — face paint sold as a solid cake that you wet with water to use; it dries on the skin and washes off with soap and water (01.1)
- **waterline** — the wet inner rim of the eyelid, inside the lashes; no makeup goes here (05.4)
- **wet edge** — the still slightly damp edge of a paint layer; the next color blends smoothly into it, but not once it has dried (08.1)
