# 05.4 · Bold eye designs, safely

Intermediate · 60 minutes. The eyes are the center of most festival faces: a sharp wing, a line
floating above the crease, dots under the brow, a flash of color on the brow bone. They are also the
part of the face that needs the most care. In this lesson you learn what may go where around the
eyes, paint four bold eye designs with eye-safe products, and finish with your first complete
festival face: the module's final practical.

## Materials

> [!CAUTION]
> Around the eyes use **only products whose label allows eye-area use**. A color that is fine on the
> cheek may not be allowed on the eyelid. **No fluorescent (neon, UV) or glow-in-the-dark paint near
> the eyes**: none is allowed there (US FDA). Nothing goes on the **waterline** (the wet inner rim of
> the lid): keep makeup outside the lash line. No glitter or gems on the lids. **Never share** eye
> makeup. Don't paint near the eyes in a moving car or bus. If an eye stings, waters or turns red,
> stop, rinse gently with clean water and remove the makeup; if pain or blurry vision continues,
> get medical help.

| Item | What it does here | Ideal | Cheaper or easier to find | It must… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eye-safe liner | The wing and the lash line | Black liquid or gel eyeliner you already use | A black eye pencil, sharpened | Be labeled for the eyes, your own, in date |
| Eye-safe face paint | Crease line, brow-bone color, dots | Face paint whose label allows eye-area use | Eye shadow (powder) in bright colors, applied with a damp brush | Be labeled for the eye area |
| Fine brushes | Lines and dots | Synthetic liner, size 0-1, and a small angled brush | Synthetic art brushes kept only for face paint | Spring back to a sharp point |

## How it works

The **eye area** runs from the brows down to the top of the cheekbones. Three rules decide what goes
where:

- **Eye area:** only products whose label allows eye-area use. No neon, UV or glow-in-the-dark paint,
  and no glitter for beginners: flakes can fall into the eye and scratch it, especially with contact
  lenses. Save neon and glitter for outside the eye area (Module 6).
- **Lash line:** eye-safe liner along the base of the lashes, on the outside only.
- **Waterline:** nothing. Makeup on the wet rim of the lid can block the tiny oil glands there and
  irritate the eye.

Four bold designs fit inside those rules. A **graphic wing** is liner along the upper lash line that
flicks out to a point; its angle follows an imaginary line from the lower lash line toward the end of
the brow. A **floating crease** is a line painted just above the natural crease (the fold of the
eyelid), so it shows with the eyes open. **Dots under the brow** follow the curve of the brow. **Brow-
bone color** is a soft blend on the bone just under the brow, sweeping out toward the temple.

Eye designs come in two kinds. An **open-eye design** (wing, floating crease, brow-bone color, dots)
is painted with the eyes open, looking straight ahead, because the lid folds away when the eyes
open. A **closed-eye design** is painted on the moving lid: it is almost hidden when the eyes are
open and only shows when they close, for photos. Festival looks are mostly open-eye designs.

**Your eyes, all day:** wear soft contact lenses? Put them in **before** makeup and take them out
**before** you remove it. Decorative contact lenses bought without a prescription can seriously
injure the eye (US FDA). Throw away eye makeup after about three months, never wet mascara with
saliva, and take everything off before you sleep, gently, without scrubs.

## Step by step

1. **Check every product's label** for eye-area use. Anything neon, UV, glow-in-the-dark or glitter
   stays out of the eye area.
2. **Prep** as in [lesson 05.2](lesson-02.md); contact lenses go in now.
3. **Sit close to the mirror**, rest your little finger on your cheek to steady your hand, eyes open,
   looking straight ahead.
4. **Find the wing angle**: imagine the lower lash line continuing toward the end of the brow. Mark
   the tip with a tiny dot of liner. Same height on both eyes.
5. **Liner and wing:** thin at the inner corner, thicker toward the outer corner, out to the dot.
   Outside the lashes, never on the waterline. Other eye straight away.
6. **Floating crease:** a thin line of eye-safe paint just above the natural crease, from the middle
   of the lid out to the wing. Both eyes.
7. **Brow-bone color or dots:** dab soft color under the brow with a sponge, or place dots along the
   curve of the brow. Both eyes.
8. **Check eyes open and closed**, step back, then set the face as in lesson 05.2 with your eyes
   closed.

## Practice exercise

**Four eye designs on paper, then the wing on your hand** (20 minutes).

1. On the `eye-designs` sheet, paint each gray design on the left eye, then mirror it on the right
   eye, starting at the gray dots.
2. On the back of your hand, practice the wing stroke ten times with eye-safe liner: a thin line that
   gets thicker, then a quick flick out to a point.
3. Look at the sheet in a mirror or a photo: do the wings point to the same height on both eyes?

Stop when the wings on your sheet mirror each other, and your last three hand flicks end in a clean,
sharp point.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Wing tip shaky? Don't pull one long line from the inner corner. Start at the tip dot and pull a short
line back toward the lash line, then join it with liner along the lashes. Short strokes are steadier.

</details>

## Mini-project

**Module 5 practical: your first festival face.** Put the whole module together on yourself in a
mirror (or on the `festival-layouts` and `eye-designs` sheets first):

1. A bold palette of two or three colors plus black and white (05.1).
2. Prep the skin, and plan the order: paint, then makeup, then setting (05.2).
3. One layout from 05.3, sketched lightly and painted outside the eye area.
4. One open-eye design from this lesson, with eye-safe products only.
5. Set it, take a photo from two or three meters away, do a rub test on the edge, and at the end of
   the day remove everything gently.

You're done when:

- [ ] Every product near the eyes is labeled for eye-area use; nothing neon, UV or glittery is near the eyes.
- [ ] Nothing is on the waterline, and the liner sits outside the lashes.
- [ ] The eye design and the layout match on both sides and look good with the eyes open and closed.
- [ ] From two or three meters away, every part of the design reads clearly.
- [ ] The design passed the rub test, and everything came off gently with eye-safe remover around the eyes.

## Common beginner mistakes

| What happens | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Floating crease vanishes when the eyes open | Painted on the moving lid with the eyes closed | Paint it with the eyes open, just above the natural crease |
| Wings point to different heights | Each wing drawn freehand without a guide | Mark both wing tips with a dot first, matched from the lower lash line |
| Eyes water and sting during the day | Liner on the waterline, or a product not labeled for eyes | Keep makeup outside the lashes; use only eye-labeled products |
| Liner smudges onto the lid fold | Liner still wet when the eyes blink | Let it dry with the eyes open, looking down, before you blink |
| Wing shaky or blobby | One long stroke from the inner corner | Start at the tip dot; short strokes back toward the lashes |

## Optional challenge

Paint a **closed-eye design** for a photo: a soft gradient of two eye-safe colors on the moving lid,
with a few dots near the crease (never on the lash line). Take two photos, eyes open and eyes closed.
How much of the design shows in each?
