# 08.2 · Making shapes look 3D

Advanced · 50 minutes. A flat circle becomes a shiny ball, a hexagon becomes a gem and a triangle
becomes a horn as soon as you add light and shadow in the right places. In this lesson you learn the
five parts of light that every painter uses, and you paint a 3D gem, a 3D horn and a twisting ribbon,
first on paper and then on your forehead.

## Materials

> [!WARNING]
> Use only face paint you have patch-tested ([lesson 01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)). The forehead
> design in the mini-project stays above the brows; keep paint off the eyelids, and close your eyes
> while you paint near the brows.

## How it works

Our eyes read **shape** from light and dark. Pick **one light direction** for the whole design, as
in [lesson 03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md): top left is easiest. Then every round shape has the
same five parts:

1. **Highlight:** the brightest spot, facing the light. White, or the color with lots of white.
2. **Midtone:** the plain color, where the light falls evenly.
3. **Shadow:** the side facing away from the light, painted with a **darker shade of the same
   color**.
4. **Reflected light:** a slightly lighter band just inside the shadow edge, where light bounces
   back. It is what makes the shape look round instead of cut in half.
5. **Cast shadow:** the soft dark shape on the skin beside the object, on the side away from the
   light.

Why not black for shadows? Black turns most colors gray and dirty. A darker shade of the same color
(dark purple under purple, dark teal under teal) keeps the shadow clean and bright. Use black only
for a thin outline, if at all.

Flat-sided shapes like gems don't blend: each **facet** (flat side) gets one value, lighter if it
faces the light, darker if it faces away. Long round shapes like horns and ribbons shade **across
their width**: light along one edge, dark along the other, all the way along.

## Step by step

### Mix the value ladder

1. Put a pool of your midtone color on the plate.
2. **Light:** pull a little of it aside and add white. **Highlight:** pure white, or white with a
   touch of the color.
3. **Shadow:** pull another part aside and darken it with a darker neighbor color (purple + a
   little blue or navy, green + a little blue, pink + a little purple). Test on paper: it should
   look like the same color in shade, not gray.

### 3D gem

1. **Sketch** the hexagon, the smaller hexagon inside it and the six joining lines, with a thin brush
   and a light color. Draw a small sun in the corner of your paper as a reminder.
2. **Fill everything** with the midtone. Let it dry.
3. **Dark facets:** paint the two or three facets on the side away from the light with the shadow
   shade; the one facing straight away is darkest.
4. **Light facets:** paint the facets facing the light with the light value, and the flat top with a
   soft change from light (near the sun) to midtone.
5. **Finish:** a thin white line along the top-left edge, a white sparkle on the top, a very thin
   dark outline, and a soft cast shadow of the darker skin or paper color on the bottom right.

### 3D horn (or ribbon)

1. **Sketch** a curved cone. Fill it with the midtone and let it dry.
2. **Shadow side:** paint a band of the shadow shade down the side away from the light. Soften its
   inner edge with a damp brush or a sponge corner while it is still damp.
3. **Light side:** paint a band of the light value down the side facing the light.
4. **Grooves:** curved lines across the horn, each a dark line with a thin light line just below it.
5. **Finish:** a long thin white shine on the light side, a thin outline, a sparkle and a cast shadow.

For a **ribbon**, paint a wavy band, make the parts where it turns over (the back of the ribbon) a
darker shade, then shade each part across its width as for the horn.

## Practice exercise

On the `value-ladder` sheet or plain paper (25 minutes):

1. Mix and paint a five-step value ladder for one color, and a three-step ladder for a second color.
2. Paint one ball with all five parts of light.
3. Paint one gem and one horn, each with a sun drawn in the corner.

Stop when someone glancing at your paper can point to where the light comes from on every shape, and
none of your shadows looks gray.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Ball looks like a flat disk with a dark crescent? You probably skipped the midtone: let the shadow
fade into the plain color over a wide zone, and keep the reflected light faint. Horn looks striped?
Soften the edge between the bands with a barely damp brush while the paint is still slightly wet.

</details>

## Mini-project

Paint the **3D unicorn horn and gem** from the face strip. First on `face-front` from
[labs/common](../../labs/common/) (or the shapes on the `value-ladder` sheet), then on your own
forehead in a mirror: the horn rises from just above the space between your brows, the gem sits at
its base, and the light comes from your top left. If you prefer, paint the horn and gem on your
forearm instead.

You're done when:

- [ ] Every highlight is on the same side, and every shadow and cast shadow on the opposite side.
- [ ] The shadows are a darker shade of the same color, not black or gray.
- [ ] The gem has at least three clearly different facet values, and the horn is shaded across its
      width with visible grooves.
- [ ] Nothing is painted on the eyelids or below the brows.

## Common beginner mistakes

| What happens | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow looks gray and dirty | Black mixed in or painted on top | Shade with a darker shade of the same color; keep black for thin outlines only |
| Shape still looks flat | Highlights on two sides, or no clear light direction | Draw a small sun on your paper; every highlight on that side, every shadow on the other |
| Ball looks cut in half | Light and dark meet with no midtone | Let the shadow fade into the midtone; add a faint reflected light |
| Gem facets blur into each other | Painted while the next facet was wet | Let each facet dry before painting the one beside it |
| Horn looks like a striped flag | Hard edges between the shadow, mid and light bands | Soften each band edge while damp, or dab it lightly with a sponge corner |

## Optional challenge

Paint a **3D ribbon** that wraps around a gem: the ribbon passes behind the gem on one side and in
front of it on the other. The part behind gets a cast shadow from the gem; the part in front casts a
shadow onto the gem.
