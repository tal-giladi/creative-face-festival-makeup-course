# 08.1 · Three-color blends and split cakes

Intermediate · 50 minutes. In Module 3 you blended two colors with a sponge. Now you blend three, so a
cheek or temple can glow from purple through pink to yellow, and you meet the split cake: one brush
load that paints several colors at once. By the end you can paint one-stroke leaves, petals and a
whole flower in a single turn of the brush, on paper and on your face.

## Materials

> [!WARNING]
> Use only face paint you have patch-tested ([lesson 01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)). Keep the
> blend outside the eye area: on the brow bone, temple and cheekbone, never on the eyelid. Split cakes
> must be sold as face paint; never load a brush from craft paint, poster paint or a watercolor set.

| Item | What it does here | Ideal | Cheaper or easier to find | It must… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wide sponge | Carries three colors side by side | High-density face-painting sponge, flat side about 5 cm wide | A makeup wedge or a beauty-blender style sponge cut in half for a flat side | Be soft, fine-pored and new |
| Wide flat brush | One-stroke petals, leaves and swirls | Synthetic flat face-painting brush, 1.5-2.5 cm ("one-stroke" brush) | A synthetic flat brush from the craft aisle, or a flat makeup brush | Have a straight, sharp edge when wet and spring back |
| Split cake (optional) | Several colors side by side in one cake | A face-painting split cake, e.g. a pink or green "petal" cake | Make your own load: paint three stripes of your normal cakes side by side on a clean plate, then load from those (see Step by step) | Be face paint |
| Face paint | The colors | A light, a middle and a dark color, e.g. yellow, pink, purple; plus white | Any three colors in a row on the color wheel ([lesson 03.5](../module-03/lesson-05.md)) | Be face paint, patch-tested |
| Spray bottle (optional) | Waking up the split cake | Fine-mist bottle | Wet the cake with a few drops from a clean brush | Hold clean water, never sprayed at a face |
| Water, white plate, paper towel | Loading and blotting | As in [lesson 03.2](../module-03/lesson-02.md) | Glasses, a white saucer | Clean water, white surface |
| Practice sheet | Blend boxes and one-stroke models | `blend-and-one-stroke` from [labs/module-08](../../labs/module-08/) | Plain thick paper, or draw the outline freehand | Not curl when damp |

## How it works

A three-color blend is just two two-color blends side by side. You load three stripes on one wide
sponge and get **two blending zones**: one where the first and second color meet, one where the
second and third meet. The trick is the **middle color**. It must be a neighbor of *both* outer colors
on the color wheel, so each zone stays clean: yellow, pink, purple works; yellow, purple, green
turns muddy ([lesson 03.1](../module-03/lesson-01.md)).

If you blend in layers instead (light color first, then the next), timing matters. Paint the next
color while the edge of the last one is still slightly damp, the **wet edge**. Wait until it is
fully dry and a hard line appears where the new color starts. Use too much water and everything runs
into mud.

A **split cake** has several colors side by side in one pot. You rub a flat brush across the
stripes and slide it back and forth **along** them, always on the same path. The brush picks up each
color on its own part of the bristles, so one stroke paints all the stripes at once: that is a
**one-stroke** design. The light corner and the dark corner of the brush are what make petals and
leaves look shaded with no extra work.

## Step by step

### Three-color sponge blend

1. **Pick three neighbors**, light to dark: yellow, pink, purple (or yellow, green, blue).
2. **Dampen a wide sponge** and squeeze it until it is only damp.
3. **Load three stripes.** Rub the left third of the flat side on the light color, the middle third
   on the middle color, the right third on the dark color. Don't rub across the stripes.
4. **Dab on the plate** three or four times in one place.
5. **Dab on the paper or skin**, always holding the sponge the same way round.
6. **Blend the two zones**: short dabs left and right over each meeting point, a few millimeters at a
   time. Fade the outer edges with lighter dabs.

### Split cake and one stroke

1. **Wake up the cake** with one or two mists of water (or a few drops from a brush) until it looks
   glossy, not puddled.
2. **Dampen the flat brush** and blot it on the paper towel.
3. **Load across, slide along.** Lay the brush across all the stripes and slide it back and forth
   along them 10-15 times, on the same path every time, until the paint sits high in the bristles.
4. **Test stroke.** Paint one straight stroke on paper. Clean stripes? Go. Muddy? See the table below.
5. **Leaf:** press the whole edge down, slide forward while turning the brush a little, and lift up
   onto the edge to finish in a point.
6. **Petal:** put the light corner where the flower center will be, press, wiggle out and back, and
   lift. Turn the paper or your wrist and repeat for five petals.
7. **Swirl:** start pressed flat, then lift onto the edge as you curl, so the line gets thinner.
8. **Rotated flower:** keep the light corner on the center and turn the brush around it in one
   movement, like the hand of a clock.

No split cake? Paint three short stripes of your normal cakes side by side on a clean plate, slide the
flat brush along them in the same way, and you have a home-made split load.

### Keeping the cake clean

- Always load in the same direction as the stripes. Never scrub in circles.
- Wipe the brush between loads if it touched another color, and rinse it fully at the end.
- If a stripe gets smudged, wipe the top layer off with a clean, damp paper towel, moving along the
  stripes, and let it dry.
- Use your own cake for yourself. If you paint other people later, load only with a clean brush, never
  a used sponge ([lesson 01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)).

## Practice exercise

On the `blend-and-one-stroke` sheet or plain paper (25 minutes):

1. Two three-color sponge blends, each about 10 x 5 cm. Choose different neighbors for each.
2. One layered blend: yellow first, pink dabbed in while the yellow edge is still damp, then purple.
3. A row of five test strokes from the split cake (or your home-made split load). They should all
   show clean stripes.
4. Five leaves, one five-petal flower and two swirls, then one rotated flower.

Stop when a three-color blend shows no hard line and no brown or gray zone, and your last five test
strokes all show clean stripes.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Petals look striped instead of shaded? Load longer: 10-15 slides along the stripes push the paint
higher into the bristles, so the colors soften into each other inside the brush. Rotated flower
smudges in the middle? Keep the light corner pressed down and still; turn your wrist, not your arm.

</details>

## Mini-project

Paint the **temple crescent with a one-stroke flower** from the face strip: a three-color sponge blend
from above the brow, around the outside of the eye and onto the cheekbone, then a five-petal
one-stroke flower on the temple, two leaves, a small swirl, a yellow center and a few white dots. Paint
it on `face-front` from [labs/common](../../labs/common/) first, then on your own face in a mirror (or on
the back of your hand if you're not ready for the face). Photograph it from arm's length.

You're done when:

- [ ] The blend has two soft zones and no muddy middle.
- [ ] Every petal is pale at the center and dark at the rim, with the same shading on all five.
- [ ] The leaves end in a point, and the split cake still shows clean stripes afterwards.
- [ ] Nothing is painted on the eyelid or between the brow and the lash line.

## Common beginner mistakes

| What happens | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A hard line inside the blend | The first color dried before the next went on | Add the next color while the edge is still slightly damp; soften an old line with a lightly loaded sponge |
| Brown or gray zone in a three-color blend | The middle color is not a neighbor of both outer colors | Choose three neighbors on the color wheel, e.g. yellow, pink, purple |
| Split cake turns muddy | The brush was rubbed across the stripes or in circles | Load across, slide along the stripes on the same path; wipe a smudged top layer off |
| Colors run and drip in the stroke | Too much water on the cake or brush | Mist once or twice only; blot the brush before loading |
| One-stroke petals look streaky with gaps | Too little paint in the brush | Slide along the stripes 10-15 times until the paint sits high in the bristles |

## Optional challenge

Make a **rainbow-to-leaf vine**: load a green split cake, paint a long curved vine stroke on your
forearm, and add one-stroke leaves along it, all with the dark edge on the same side (the shadow
side, as in [lesson 03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md)). Finish with one rotated flower at the end.
