# 08.3 · Scales, lace and stencils

Advanced · 60 minutes. Mermaid and dragon scales and fine lace look like hours of work, but they
are built from tiny repeated shapes. A stencil prints them in seconds; your brush can paint them
freehand with a few simple strokes. In this lesson you use a stencil safely, make your own from a
plastic folder, fishnet or lace, paint scales and lace by hand, and put both on a face.

## Materials

> [!CAUTION]
> **No stencils over the eyes.** A stencil can slip and the sponge pushes paint under it, so keep
> stencils on the temples, cheekbones, forehead and body, never across the eyelids or the eye area.
> Close your eyes whenever you work near the brows. Cutting stencils with a craft knife is a job for
> an adult, on a cutting mat, away from the face.

| Item | What it does here | Ideal | Cheaper or easier to find | It must… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stencil | Prints scales, lace or stars in one go | A reusable face-painting stencil (thin, flexible plastic, often self-adhesive) | Cut your own from a thin plastic folder or clean food packaging (sheet `stencil-cutouts` in [labs/module-08](../../labs/module-08/)); a piece of **new, washed** fishnet stocking or lace fabric | Be thin enough to lie flat, washable, with no sharp edges, and used only for face paint |
| Sponge | Dabbing paint through the stencil | A low-density or small face-painting sponge | A makeup wedge, cut into small pieces | Be soft and new; one per person |
| Round brushes, size 1-3 | Freehand scales, lace, highlights | Synthetic face-painting round and liner brushes | Synthetic watercolor brushes | Spring back to a fine point |
| Face paint | Base, stencil color, lines | A base blend (e.g. teal, blue, purple), a dark color for scales and white for lace | Any colors from your kit | Be face paint, patch-tested |

## How it works

A **stencil** is a thin sheet with holes. Paint goes through the holes and the sheet blocks the rest,
so every print has the same sharp shapes. Two things make it crisp: the sheet must lie **flat and
still**, and the sponge must be **nearly dry**. Wet paint creeps under the edges and the shapes go
fuzzy. Dab from the edges of the holes **inward**, so you never push paint under the sheet, then lift
it **straight up**. Any slide smears the print.

Fishnet stockings and lace fabric work as soft stencils: the paint passes through the open holes of
the net and the threads stay skin-colored, so a blend turns into diamond scales or a lace pattern.
Use a piece that is **new and washed**, and keep it for face paint only.

Freehand **scales** are rows of small "U" shapes, which are C-strokes turned on their side. Each new
row starts in the gap between two U shapes below, like bricks in a wall. To make them look 3D, use
[lesson 08.2](lesson-02.md): each scale is a little darker at the top (it is tucked under the row
above) and gets a white highlight on its curved bottom edge.

Freehand **lace** is four simple elements: scallops (joined round curves), small loops inside them,
dots at the points, and a fine net above with tiny dot flowers ([lesson 02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)).

## Step by step

### Stencil scales

1. **Base first.** Sponge a two- or three-color base ([lesson 08.1](lesson-01.md)) and let it dry
   completely.
2. **Nearly dry sponge.** Load a small sponge piece, then dab it on the plate or a paper towel until
   it leaves only a dry, even print.
3. **Place the stencil flat** on the area and hold it with two fingers (or press down a
   self-adhesive one). Keep it well away from the eye.
4. **Dab from the edges in**, lightly, two or three times per spot. Don't wipe.
5. **Lift straight up** in one movement. Move the stencil to the next spot only when the first print
   is dry, and line up the scales by eye.
6. **Highlight** a few scales with a short white curve on their bottom edge.

### DIY stencils

1. **Plastic folder:** print `stencil-cutouts`, cut out one card, and tape it on top of a piece of a
   thin, clear plastic folder. An adult cuts through both along the solid lines with small scissors
   or a craft knife on a cutting mat. Remove the paper, smooth any sharp corners, and wash the
   stencil before first use.
2. **Fishnet or lace:** cut a piece about 15 x 15 cm from a **new** stocking or lace fabric and wash
   it with soap. Stretch it flat over the area (a second pair of hands, or your own fingers at the
   edges), dab with a nearly dry sponge, lift straight off.

### Freehand scales

1. **Base**, dry.
2. **Bottom row:** a row of U strokes with a round brush, each one pressed in the middle and thin at
   the ends ([lesson 02.1](../module-02/lesson-01.md)), touching at their tips.
3. **Next rows up:** start each U in the gap between two U shapes below.
4. **Shade:** a thin wash of a darker shade at the top of each scale.
5. **Highlight:** a short white curve inside the bottom of each scale, all on the same side.

### Freehand lace

1. **Scallops:** a row of round U curves joined at their points, along a gentle curve.
2. **Loops:** a small circle or loop inside each scallop.
3. **Dots:** a dot at each point and a small hanging dot under each scallop.
4. **Net and flowers:** thin crossing lines above the scallops, then a five-dot flower in some gaps.

### Clean stencils

Wash every stencil straight after use, before the paint dries: soak it in soapy water, rub gently
between your fingers, rinse, and press it dry between paper towels. Keep your stencils for yourself
or wash them between people, like brushes.

## Practice exercise

On the `scales-lace-practice` sheet or paper (30 minutes):

1. One patch of freehand scales, 6 rows, with shading and highlights.
2. One lace border of 6 scallops with loops, dots, net and flowers.
3. Cut one stencil from the `stencil-cutouts` sheet (or use fishnet) and print it three times on
   paper: once with a damp sponge and twice with a nearly dry one. Compare the edges.
4. Print the stencil once on your forearm over a dry base.

Stop when your nearly-dry prints have sharp edges and your freehand scale rows sit like bricks, with
no scale stacked directly on top of the one below.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Scale rows drifting crooked? Lightly sketch a few horizontal guide curves with a pale color first,
one per row, and start each row at the same side. Lace looks heavy? Use the smallest brush for the
net and keep the lines very thin; only the scallops and dots are bold.

</details>

## Mini-project

Paint **mermaid scales and lace** from the face strip. First on `face-front` from
[labs/common](../../labs/common/), then on yourself in a mirror: a sponged base in a curve from the temple around the outside of the eye onto
the cheekbone, stencil scales on the temple and cheekbone (or freehand ones), white highlights, and a
band of freehand lace across the forehead above the brows. Painting one side only is fine.

You're done when:

- [ ] The stencil scales have sharp edges, with no smears or doubled edges.
- [ ] Freehand rows are offset like bricks, darker at the top of each scale and highlighted on the
      same side.
- [ ] The lace has scallops, loops, dots and a fine net, and the net lines are thinner than the
      scallops.
- [ ] No stencil and no paint went over the eyelids, and the stencil was washed afterwards.

## Common beginner mistakes

| What happens | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stencil shapes fuzzy and blurred | Sponge too wet | Dab the sponge on a paper towel until it prints dry and even |
| Print smeared in one direction | Stencil slid when lifting | Hold it still and lift straight up in one movement |
| Every shape has a doubled edge | Stencil moved while dabbing | Hold it with two fingers or use a self-adhesive stencil; dab, don't rub |
| Freehand scales look like a grid | Each row stacked straight above the last | Start each U in the gap between two U shapes below |
| Lace looks thick and messy | Net painted with a big brush or too much paint | Smallest brush, creamy paint, light touch for the net |
