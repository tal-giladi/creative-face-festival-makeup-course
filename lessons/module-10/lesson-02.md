# 10.2 · Hands and arms

Intermediate · 50 minutes. This optional lesson is part of Module 10; the core course is complete
without it. Hands and forearms are the easiest body area to paint on yourself, so you will learn to
paint bracelets and vines that wrap all the way around, working on your own non-dominant hand and
arm with no helper.

## Materials

> [!WARNING]
> Only patch-tested face and body paint ([lesson 01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)), never on cuts,
> scratches, rashes or sunburned skin. Painted hands touch everything: **wash your hands before you
> eat** and keep painted fingers away from your mouth and eyes. If you add glitter, use it only in a
> gel on the back of the hand, never loose near the face, and don't rub your eyes with a glittery hand
> ([lesson 06.2](../module-06/lesson-02.md)). Gems on hands catch and fall off: keep them away from
> small children.

## How it works

**Paint your non-dominant side.** If you are right-handed, paint your left hand and arm (and the other
way round). It feels strange for five minutes and then becomes easy, because the painting hand does
what it always does.

**Turn the arm, not the brush.** A wrist and forearm are round. You can only paint well on the part
that faces you. So paint the top, then **turn the arm** (palm down, thumb up, palm up) and continue
on the next part, resting the arm on a rolled towel each time. The painting hand stays in its normal,
comfortable grip. Let each part dry for a moment before you turn it, so the wet paint doesn't touch
the towel.

**Wrap, don't stick.** A **bracelet** is a band that goes all the way round the wrist, just above the
wrist bone. A **wrap** is a vine or band that spirals around the forearm: it leaves one edge of the
arm and comes back on the other. Even if nobody sees the underside, painting the line round makes the
design look like it belongs to the arm.

**Where it stays.** The back of the hand, the backs of the fingers and the outer forearm stay neat
for hours. The **palm** and the **inner sides of the fingers** touch everything, sweat and get
washed, so paint there rubs off within minutes. Stop the design at the sides of the hand. Paint also
cracks if it is thick over a bend, so keep the wrist crease and the knuckles to thin lines.

**Mirror tips.** You don't need a mirror for the back of your own hand, but it helps for the outer
forearm and elbow side: hold the arm so you see it in the mirror, then paint looking at the arm
itself, not at the reflection. Use the mirror or your phone camera to check that a bracelet lines up
all the way round.

**Washing hands.** Painted hands still need washing before you eat and after the toilet. Wash palms
and fingertips carefully and pat the back of the hand dry; some color may come off, so pack a small
touch-up kit ([lesson 05.2](../module-05/lesson-02.md)). At the end of the day, warm water and soap
take it all off.

## Step by step

1. **Set up:** sit at a table, non-dominant forearm on a rolled towel, palm down. Good light.
2. **Lead lines:** with a light color, a curve all the way round the wrist just above the wrist bone,
   a line up to the back of the hand, and a vine spiraling down the forearm.
3. **Vines:** paint the stems on top. Let them dry for a moment.
4. **Turn thumb up:** continue the bracelet and the vine on the side. Let it dry.
5. **Turn palm up:** close the bracelet and finish the vine on the inner forearm. Stop at the sides of
   the hand; leave the palm empty.
6. **Turn back palm down:** leaves along every vine, on alternate sides, pointing the same way.
7. **Flower** on the back of the hand, between the knuckles and the wrist.
8. **Details:** veins, outlines, white highlights, dot trails. Thin lines only over the wrist crease.
9. **Check** all the way round in a mirror or with your phone camera.
10. **Remove** later with warm water and mild soap ([lesson 01.5](../module-01/lesson-05.md)).

## Practice exercise

**Bracelet drill, then the wrap** (25 minutes).

1. On the `vine-bracelet` sheet in a sheet protector, trace the vine, leaves and flower once
   (5 minutes).
2. On your non-dominant wrist, paint the three bracelets from the picture one after another: leaf,
   drop, swirl cuff. Wash each off before the next (12 minutes).
3. Paint one vine that wraps all the way round your forearm in three turns, with leaves
   (8 minutes).

Stop when your bracelet closes all the way round with no gap or step, and the vine on the inner
forearm meets the vine on top.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

If the bracelet does not meet itself, put three tiny dots round the wrist first (top, thumb side,
underside) at the same distance from the wrist bone, and paint from dot to dot.

</details>

## Mini-project

**A vine or bracelet that wraps the wrist and forearm.** On your own non-dominant hand and forearm,
paint the vine bracelet from the step strip (or your arm plan from [lesson 10.1](lesson-01.md)): a
bracelet all the way round the wrist, a flower on the back of the hand, and a vine that spirals down
the forearm. Photograph it from the top and from the side.

You're done when:

- [ ] The bracelet goes all the way round, just above the wrist bone, with no gap.
- [ ] The vine leaves one edge of the forearm and comes back on the other.
- [ ] The palm and the inner sides of the fingers are empty, and only thin lines cross the wrist crease.
- [ ] You painted it alone, turning your arm instead of twisting your painting hand.
- [ ] You washed your hands before eating, and the skin is calm after removal.

## Common beginner mistakes

| What happens | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Your painting hand twists and the lines wobble | Reaching round the arm instead of turning it | Turn the arm on a rolled towel; paint only the part facing you |
| The bracelet ends in a step where it meets | Painted freehand all the way round | Three guide dots at the same height, then paint dot to dot |
| Smudges after turning the arm | The wet part touched the towel | Let each part dry for a moment before you turn |
| Paint on the palm gone within minutes | The palm touches and sweats | Stop the design at the sides of the hand |
| A band cracks at the wrist | Thick paint over the bend | Thin layers; keep bands just above the wrist bone |

## Optional challenge

Paint a **sleeve band** on both forearms at once, mirrored: the same vine spiraling down the left
arm and up the right, so that when you hold your wrists together the two bracelets line up like one
design.
