# 07.2 · Geometric lines

Intermediate · 50 minutes. Clean triangles, chevrons and diamonds give a festival face a sharp,
modern look, and they are easier than they seem once you stop trying to paint a long line in one go.
In this lesson you paint straight lines on a curved face from anchor dots with short strokes, build
four geometric shapes, and place a symmetrical geometric design on the face map.

## Materials

> [!WARNING]
> Use only patch-tested face paint ([lesson 01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)). Geometric lines often
> tempt you toward the eyes: keep every line, triangle and chevron outside the eye area, or use only
> paint whose label allows eye-area use ([lesson 05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)). Never draw
> guide lines on skin with a pen, marker or ruler edge.

| Item | What it does here | Ideal | Cheaper or easier to find | It must… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Liner brush | Thin, even straight lines | Synthetic liner or round, size 0-1 | A size 1-2 synthetic watercolor brush with a sharp point | Spring back to a sharp point and hold enough paint for a short stroke |
| Flat or round brush | Filling triangles and diamonds | Synthetic flat, 6-10 mm, or round size 4 | A small concealer brush | Have a clean edge or point for crisp corners |
| Face paint | Color fills, black lines, white dots | Your bold palette ([lesson 05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md)) plus black and white | Kids' face paint | Be face paint; black mixed like ink for lines |
| Light sketching color | Anchor dots | A light face paint with a fine brush | A white or light brown cosmetic pencil | Be a cosmetic, never a pen or marker |
| Guide sheet | Practice | `geometric-guides` from [labs/module-07](../../labs/module-07/) | `festival-face-map` from [labs/module-05](../../labs/module-05/), or an oval with a center line and brow line | Show the center line and eye area |
| Paper | Shape drills | Plain or printer paper | The back of a cereal box | Be smooth |
| Dotting tool | Corner dots | A small dotting tool | The end of a brush handle | Have a smooth round end |

No printer? Draw an oval face with a center line and level lines through the brows and cheekbones,
or draw the outline freehand. The full list is in
[Materials and substitutes](../../references/materials.md).

## How it works

A long straight line is hard on any surface and harder on a face, which curves away from you in
every direction. Professionals don't paint long lines in one stroke. They do two things:

1. **Anchor dots first.** Mark every corner and line end with a tiny light dot, on both sides of
   the face, before any line. Now you only have to join dots, and you can check the symmetry
   before anything is painted.
2. **Short connected strokes.** Join two dots with two or three short strokes, each one starting
   exactly where the last one ended. Load the liner well, keep the brush upright and pull toward
   yourself; turn your hand (or the paper) so you never push the brush.

Every shape in this lesson is built from anchor dots:

- **Triangle:** three corner dots. Point it up or down; a smaller triangle inside makes it pop.
- **Chevrons:** a stack of V shapes, three dots each, the same size and the same gap. On the
  cheekbones they point up and out toward the temple.
- **Diamond:** four dots on a cross. Split it into four triangles in a light and a dark color and it
  looks like a cut gem.
- **Star in a circle:** six dots round a circle like 12, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 on a clock. Join every
  second dot to make one triangle, then the other three to make a second: a six-point star. This is
  the simple start of the "sacred geometry" style seen at festivals: circles, triangles and stars
  built from evenly spaced dots.

On the face, geometric designs follow the festival face map: one shape on the **center line**
(usually a diamond or star on the third eye), mirrored lines over the brows toward the temples, and
chevrons along the cheekbones. Fill colors first, black lines over the edges second, white dots last.

A small tilt on one side is far more visible in geometric designs than in flowers or swirls.

## Step by step

1. **Plan on the face map:** one center shape, brow lines, cheekbone chevrons. Keep the eye area
   empty.
2. **Anchor dots, both sides:** diamond corners on the center line, then the ends of each line and
   the chevron points on the left, then the same points on the right. Check in the mirror.
3. **Linework:** with the liner and black (or a dark color), join the dots with short strokes. Center
   shape first, then one side, then the same line on the other side.
4. **Fill:** color the triangles and diamond facets with a flat or round brush, staying just inside
   the lines. Let them dry.
5. **Clean the edges:** go over the black lines once more where the fill crossed them.
6. **Chevrons:** on each cheekbone, three V shapes of the same size, pointing up and out toward the
   temple. Both sides.
7. **Finish:** white dots on corners and line ends, and a small accent on the center line.

## Practice exercise

**Straight lines and shapes on paper, then on your arm** (25 minutes).

1. On paper, dot two points 6 cm apart and join them with three short strokes. Repeat ten times,
   turning the paper so you always pull toward yourself.
2. Paint each of the four shapes from the demonstration: anchor dots, lines, fill, outline.
3. On the `geometric-guides` sheet, trace the gray left half, then mirror it on the right half using
   only the anchor dots.
4. On your inner forearm, paint one diamond and a stack of three chevrons.

Stop when your straight lines have no visible wobble, the corners meet exactly at the dots, and the
right half of the guide sheet matches the left from arm's length.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Lines bulging? The paint is too thin and the brush is dragging. Mix the black like ink, load the
liner fully, and lift at the end of each short stroke instead of stopping with pressure.

</details>

## Mini-project

**A symmetrical geometric design.** Paint it on the `geometric-guides` sheet or the `face-front`
sheet from [labs/common](../../labs/common/), then on your own face in a mirror: a center shape on
the third eye, mirrored lines or triangles over both brows, and chevrons on both cheekbones.

You're done when:

- [ ] Every corner and line end started as an anchor dot, on both sides.
- [ ] Lines are straight and even, and the corners meet.
- [ ] The center shape sits exactly on the center line.
- [ ] Both sides match in height and angle, and nothing is inside the eye area.
- [ ] From two meters away the shapes still read clearly.

## Common beginner mistakes

| What happens | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wobbly lines | One long stroke across the curve of the face | Anchor dots, then two or three short connected strokes |
| Corners don't meet | Lines painted without anchor dots | Dot every corner first and stroke from dot to dot |
| One side higher or tilted | Each side painted freehand by eye | Place all anchor dots on both sides first and check before painting |
| Fill floods over the lines | Black lines painted first and the fill too wet | Fill first, let it dry, black lines over the edges last |
| Design creeps into the eye area | Lines and chevrons placed by eye near the eyes | Keep the eye area empty on the plan; chevrons on the cheekbone, pointing to the temple |

## Optional challenge

Paint a **flower of life** style piece on paper: a circle, six equal circles whose centers sit on
the first circle at 12, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10, and a ring around them all. Use a coin or bottle cap as a
template on paper only, then fill alternate petals with two colors.
