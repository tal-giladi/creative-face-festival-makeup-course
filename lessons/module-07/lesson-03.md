# 07.3 · Psychedelic waves and swirls

Intermediate · 50 minutes. Psychedelic festival faces are made of lines that flow: rainbow bands
that rise from between the brows, curl at the temples and ripple along the cheekbones. The secret is
one good lead line and then patient copying. In this lesson you nest lines at an even gap, turn them
into color bands, place them so they follow the face, and use opposite colors that seem to vibrate.

## Materials

> [!WARNING]
> Use only patch-tested face paint ([lesson 01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)). Swirls that curl
> near the brows must stay above the brow bone and outside the eye area unless the paint's label
> allows eye-area use ([lesson 05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)). If you use UV/neon paint for the
> bands, keep it far from the eyes ([lesson 06.4](../module-06/lesson-04.md)).

## How it works

**One lead line, then copies.** Every wave design starts with a single **lead line**: the first,
most important flowing line. Paint it slowly and smoothly; everything else copies it. The next line
runs beside it at the same distance, like a second lane on a road, and the next copies that one.
This is **nesting**. Copy the line next to you, not the lead line far away, and keep the gap the
same all along.

**Lines into bands.** The lanes between nested lines become **color bands**. Rainbow order
(yellow, orange, magenta, purple, or any neighbors on the color wheel from
[lesson 03.5](../module-03/lesson-05.md)) always flows. Paint the bands first, let them dry, then go
over every edge with a thin black line and add a thin white highlight in the lightest band. The black
lines are what keep a wave design readable from far away.

**Follow the face.** Waves look best when they follow the face's own curves: rising from between
the brows over the brow, curling at the temple, and running along the cheekbone. A **swirl** is a
wave that rolls into a spiral at its end ([lesson 02.3](../module-02/lesson-03.md)). Bands go on the
outside of the curl, so they never crowd into the middle. Waves painted straight across the face,
over the nose and eyes, fight its shape and look like stripes on a mask.

**Optical vibration.** Two strong, opposite colors (purple and yellow, blue and orange, red and
green) placed side by side in thin bands seem to shimmer and move. That "vibration" is the
psychedelic effect. Too much of it is tiring and blurry, so keep it to one area, use bands at least a
brush-width wide, and separate them with thin black lines.

gap. It makes the whole shape look bigger and more finished with almost no effort.

Use vibration as a spice, in one area of the design, and frame it with black lines.

## Step by step

1. **Sketch the lead line** lightly: from between the brows, up over the brow, curling at the
   temple. Then the same on the other side. Check from a distance.
2. **First band:** the lightest color right beside the lead line, on the outside of the curl. Both
   sides.
3. **Next bands:** each one beside the last, the same width, in rainbow order. Both sides, band by
   band. Let them dry.
4. **Cheekbone wave:** a second, shorter lead line from the temple along the cheekbone, with two or
   three bands below it.
5. **Black lines** over every edge between bands, thicker on the outside edge.
6. **Echo line:** one thin contrasting line following the outside edge at a small gap.
7. **Highlights and dots:** a thin white line in the lightest band, white dots along the outside,
   a dot in each curl and an accent on the center line.

## Practice exercise

**Nesting drill, then a band on your arm** (25 minutes), on the `wave-drill` sheet.

1. Paint over each gray lead line with the liner, then nest four lines beside it, each copying the
   one before at the same gap.
2. On two of the rows, fill the lanes with four colors in rainbow order, let them dry and add black
   lines and a white highlight.
3. Paint one row with purple and yellow only, once without black lines and once with them. Compare
   from two meters away.
4. On your inner forearm, paint a short wave of three bands with black lines.

Stop when the gaps between your nested lines stay even from start to end, and the band edges are
clean after the black lines.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Gaps getting wider or narrower? You are copying the lead line instead of the line next to you. Rest
your little finger on the paper and watch the gap, not the brush tip.

</details>

## Mini-project

**A wave-and-swirl pattern that flows with the face.** On the `face-front` sheet, then on your own
face in a mirror: a swirl from between the brows over each brow, curling at the temple, plus a
cheekbone wave on each side, with at least three color bands, black lines, an echo line and white
details.

You're done when:

- [ ] Both swirls start at the center line and curl at the same height on both temples.
- [ ] The gaps between bands stay even, and the bands sit on the outside of each curl.
- [ ] Every band edge has a clean black line, and the design reads from two meters away.
- [ ] Nothing crosses the nose or goes into the eye area.

## Common beginner mistakes

| What happens | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Gaps between nested lines grow or shrink | Copying the lead line instead of the line next to you | Copy the nearest line; watch the gap |
| Waves look like stripes on a mask | Bands painted straight across the face | Follow the brows, temples and cheekbones |
| Swirl center becomes a dark blob | Bands placed on the inside of a tight curl | Bands on the outside of the curl; keep the curl open |
| Design looks muddy from far away | Many thin lines in similar colors, no black lines | Fewer, wider bands in clear colors, with black lines between them |
| Opposite colors hurt the eyes | Thin vibrating bands over the whole face | Keep vibration in one area and frame it with black lines |

## Optional challenge

Paint a **closed swirl panel** on your forearm: a spiral of nested bands that turns twice round its
center, then add a second smaller spiral that tucks into the first, like two waves rolling into each
other. Keep every band the same width.
