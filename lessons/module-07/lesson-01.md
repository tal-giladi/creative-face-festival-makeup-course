# 07.1 · Dotwork and mandalas

Intermediate · 50 minutes. Dotwork is the easiest way to make a festival face look detailed and
"finished": rings of dots, petals and trails that grow from one center like a flower. In this lesson
you learn to keep dots even, place a ring of dots by quarters, and build a dot mandala ring by ring.
You finish with a mandala on the forehead and dot arcs on both cheekbones.

## Materials

> [!WARNING]
> Use only face paint you have patch-tested ([lesson 01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)). Dotting
> tools touch the skin many times: wash them with soap and water before and after, use a fresh cotton
> swab each time, and never use a pen, toothpick or craft tool. The mandala and the cheekbone arcs
> stay outside the eye area ([lesson 05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)).

| Item | What it does here | Ideal | Cheaper or easier to find | It must… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dotting tools | Small, medium and big dots | A double-ended dotting tool (metal balls of two sizes) | Nail-art dotting tools; the round end of a brush handle; a new cotton swab for big dots | Have a smooth round end, be washable (or new and used once) and kept only for face paint |
| Round brush | Tiny dots and petals (dot drags) | Synthetic round, size 2-4 | Synthetic watercolor brush | Come to one sharp point |
| Face paint | The colors | Three bright colors plus white, for example magenta, purple and cyan | Kids' face paint | Be face paint, mixed a little more liquid than for strokes |
| Palette | A small pool of wet paint to dip into | White palette | A white plate or plastic lid | Be white and smooth |
| Light sketching color | Marking the center | A light face paint with a fine brush | A white or light brown cosmetic pencil | Be a cosmetic, never a pen or marker |
| Mandala grids | Practice | `dot-mandala-grids` from [labs/module-07](../../labs/module-07/) | A circle traced around a cup, divided by folding the paper in half, then in quarters | Show a center and even spokes |
| Mirror | Painting your own forehead | A stand-up mirror at face height | Any mirror propped on a table | Leave both hands free |

No printer? Trace a cup, fold the paper in half twice to find the spokes, or draw the outline
freehand. The full list is in [Materials and substitutes](../../references/materials.md).

## How it works

A **mandala** is a round design that grows from one center in rings, the same in every direction,
like a flower or a snowflake. On the face it usually sits on the forehead center (the third eye on
the festival face map from [lesson 05.3](../module-05/lesson-03.md)).

Three things make dotwork look professional:

1. **Even dots.** A dot's size depends on the tool and on how much paint is on it. For a ring, dip
   again before **every** dot, the same depth each time. Dot trails that shrink are the one place you
   don't reload ([lesson 02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)).
2. **Placement by quarters.** Don't go round the circle one dot after another: the gaps drift and the
   last gap never fits. Place the top and bottom dots, then the two sides, then one halfway in each
   gap. 4 becomes 8, 8 becomes 16, always even.
3. **Ring by ring, center outward.** Center dot, a ring of small dots, then petals, then bigger dots,
   then tiny ones. Each ring lines up with the one before: either on the same **spoke** (an imagined
   line from the center out) or exactly between two spokes.

A **petal** in dotwork is a **dot drag**: place a dot, then pull the brush tip or a dotting tool
out of it toward the center, so it leaves a tail. It looks like a teardrop
([lesson 02.2](../module-02/lesson-02.md)), painted with a dot.

Shrinking dots, one big gap and wobbly rings are the three things that make dotwork look homemade.

## Step by step

1. **Mix the paint** a little more liquid than for strokes, like melted ice cream, and make a small
   pool of each color on the palette.
2. **Mark the center** with a tiny light dot. On the face: on the center line, about halfway between
   the brows and the hairline.
3. **Center dot:** the biggest tool, one dip, straight down and straight up.
4. **First ring by quarters:** top, bottom, left, right, then halfway between. Dip before every dot.
5. **Petals:** between the first-ring dots, place a dot and drag its tail toward the center.
6. **Outer rings:** big dots at the petal tips, small dots on the spokes, tiny dots outside.
   Reload before every dot.
7. **Trails and highlights:** a trail of three dots from one dip on each petal line, getting smaller,
   then a tiny white dot on the big dots and the center.
8. **Cheekbone arcs (face):** dot one arc on the left cheekbone, then the matching arc on the right
   straight away, big dots under the eye, smaller toward the temple. Then a second, smaller arc
   below.

## Practice exercise

**Even rings on paper, then on your arm** (25 minutes), on the `dot-mandala-grids` sheet.

1. Trace the gray model mandala with your tools and paint.
2. On the 8-spoke grid, paint a ring of 8 even dots by quarters, then a second ring of 8 between the
   spokes. Repeat on the 16-spoke grid with 16 dots.
3. On the 12-spoke grid, paint a mandala of your own: center, ring, petals, outer ring.
4. On your inner forearm, paint one small ring of 8 dots around a center dot.

Stop when the dots in each ring are the same size, the gaps look the same all round, and nothing
smears when you touch the dried dots lightly.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Dots growing small toward the end of a ring? You forgot to reload. Dots with a peak in the middle?
The paint is too thick: add a drop of water. Dots spreading into each other? Too wet, or too close.

</details>

## Mini-project

**Forehead mandala with cheekbone dot arcs.** Paint it on the `face-front` sheet from
[labs/common](../../labs/common/) first, then on your own face in a mirror:

1. Pick three bright colors plus white.
2. A mandala on the forehead center with at least three rings.
3. A matching dot arc on each cheekbone, sweeping up and out toward the temple, plus a second
   smaller arc.
4. Check from two meters away in the mirror or a photo.

You're done when:

- [ ] The mandala's center sits on the center line, and each ring is even all the way round.
- [ ] The dots in each ring are the same size, round, with no tails.
- [ ] The cheekbone arcs match on both sides and stay below the eye area.
- [ ] From two meters away the design reads as one round mandala and two clean arcs.

## Common beginner mistakes

| What happens | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dots get smaller around the ring | Dipped once for several dots | Reload before every dot, the same depth each time |
| The last gap in a ring is too big or too small | Dots placed one after another round the circle | Place by quarters: opposite dots first, then halfway |
| Mandala looks lopsided | No center mark or guide circle | Mark the center, imagine the circle and cross before the first ring |
| Rings look messy and crowded | Rings not lined up with each other | Put each ring on the spokes or exactly between them |
| Dots smear on the face | Next ring painted into wet dots | Let each ring dry for a minute before the next one |

## Optional challenge

Paint a **half mandala** (a "sunrise") along the hairline: the center sits on the hairline, and the
rings spread down onto the forehead in a half circle. Place the dots by quarters on the half circle:
left end, right end, top, then halfway between.
