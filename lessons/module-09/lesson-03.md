# 09.3 · Fantasy characters

Advanced · 85 minutes. A fantasy character is a design that turns you into someone else: a fairy, a dragon, a forest spirit. In this lesson you learn what makes a character read instantly (a few key features, a color story and one strong texture), practice the three textures fantasy faces use most, and paint a forest spirit step by step. Then you paint a character of your choice on your own face.

## Materials

> [!CAUTION]
> Characters often reach toward the eyes, so the rules stay the same as in every module: **eye-safe
> products only in the eye area**, nothing on the lash line or waterline, **no glitter, gems or
> neon paint near the eyes** ([lesson 05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md),
> [lesson 06.4](../module-06/lesson-04.md)). Use only face paint, cosmetic glitter and skin-safe
> adhesives, never craft products, and patch-test each new product
> ([lesson 01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md)). Scale stencils and lace fabric are held on the cheek or
> temple, never pressed onto the eyelid.

## How it works

### A character is features, a color story and a texture

People recognize a character from very little. Use the method of [lesson 09.1](lesson-01.md) and
keep each character to **3-5 key features**:

- **Fairy:** wings on the temples and upper cheeks, flowers, sparkle.
- **Dragon:** scales, horns, a crest of spikes, a sharp liner flick.
- **Forest spirit:** leaves, branches like small antlers, berries and flowers.

The **color story** is the palette plus its mood ([lesson 03.5](../module-03/lesson-05.md)): soft
pink, lilac and aqua feel light and magical; emerald, teal and gold feel strong and shiny; leaf
green, bark brown and berry red feel wild and natural. Write three mood words next to the colors
and check every choice against them.

One **main texture** makes the character believable. Pick one and give it the most space:

- **Wing veins:** lines that all start from one point near the body of the wing, a darker edge,
  white dots along the outside.
- **Scales:** rows of U shapes with a dark edge and a light curve inside, painted freehand row by
  row or sponged through a stencil ([lesson 08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)). Fade them into the
  skin at the edges so they look like they grow out of it.
- **Leaves and bark:** two-tone leaves in one stroke ([lesson 08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)) with
  a light center vein; branches dark on one side and light on the other, the 3D trick from
  [lesson 08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md).

Finally, **place it on the face**: the main texture on the temples, cheekbones or forehead, the eye
area clear except for eye-safe liner, and one focal point (horns, a flower, a gem) near the center.

### Choose themes with respect

Some designs belong to living cultures and carry meaning that a costume can't. For example, the
decorated skulls of Mexico's Día de los Muertos honor family members who have died, and the face
markings of Indigenous peoples can be sacred; the Smithsonian's National Museum of the American
Indian says plainly that dressing up as Native Americans is never appropriate. So don't copy them as
a costume; choose invented characters like the ones in this lesson, or learn from that culture's own
artists and follow their lead.

## Step by step

The forest spirit, as in the strip:

1. **Plan.** On the `mood-palette` planner: features (leaves, branches, berries, flowers), color story, three mood words.
2. **Sketch.** A light branch line from each inner brow up to the hairline; a vine line from the brow tail around the outside of the eye area down to the cheekbone.
3. **Moss.** Sponge lime and moss green on the temples and along the hairline, darker toward the outside. Fade the inner edge.
4. **Branches.** Brown with a round brush, then a darker brown down one side and a light line down the other. Add two small side branches.
5. **Leaves.** Two-tone leaves along the vine and on the branches, tips pointing outward and down. A dark green vine line connects them and ends in a curl on the cheek.
6. **Details.** Five-dot flowers (white base on deep skin, then cream), red berries with a white shine dot, lime dots trailing off the vine.
7. **Focal point.** A small leaf crown at the center of the hairline; a gem in the middle if you like. Eye-safe liner only near the eyes.

## Practice exercise

**Three textures, then one character on the template** (40 minutes).

1. On paper, paint each texture from the demonstration twice: a wing with veins, a 6 x 6 cm patch of
   scales (freehand, row by row from the bottom up, then with a stencil if you have one), and a
   branch with four two-tone leaves. 20 minutes.
2. Choose fairy, dragon or forest spirit. Paint it on the `fantasy-character` or `fairy-and-spirit`
   sheet in a sheet protector. 20 minutes.

Then paint a small patch of your character's texture on your forearm. Stop when the texture looks
even and fades softly into the skin at its edges.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Scales look even when every row is the same height: dot the start of each row first, like the
anchor dots of [lesson 07.2](../module-07/lesson-02.md). Paint from the bottom row up so each new
row overlaps the tops of the one below.

</details>

## Mini-project

**A fantasy character on your own face.** Pick one of the three, or invent your own with the
planner. For the fully guided dragon, paint
[Project 10 · Fantasy character](../../projects/p10-fantasy-character.md).

You're done when:

- [ ] Your planner shows 3-5 features, a color story and three mood words.
- [ ] One main texture (veins, scales or leaves) is clean and fades into the skin at its edges.
- [ ] Someone can name the character in one second from about three meters.
- [ ] The eye area has only eye-safe products; no glitter, gems or neon near the eyes.
- [ ] The theme is an invented character, not a sacred or cultural design worn as a costume.

## Common beginner mistakes

| What happens | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody can tell what the character is | Too many features or none that stand out | 3-5 features; make one of them big |
| Scales look like a fishnet stocking on the face | Texture everywhere, hard edges | Scales in one area only; fade the edges into the skin |
| Colors fight each other | No color story | Choose 3-4 colors and three mood words; drop anything that doesn't fit |
| Rows of scales slope or drift | Rows painted freehand without guides | Dot the start of every row; paint from the bottom row up |
| Bright colors look dull on deep skin | Painted straight on dark skin | A thin white layer under light colors first |

## Optional challenge

Design a **mash-up character**: take one feature from each of two characters (dragon scales with
fairy wings, or forest leaves with dragon horns) and give it a new color story. Draw three
thumbnails first, then paint the clearest one.
