# 09.1 · From idea to face design

Intermediate · 55 minutes. Every great fantasy face starts as a single word, like "ocean", "dragon" or "moon". In this lesson you learn a simple way to turn that word into a design you can paint: pick the key features, simplify them into brush shapes, choose a palette, sketch three tiny versions and keep the one that reads best from across the room. You finish with a design on the face map and a written plan.

## How it works

Beginners often start painting and hope a design appears. Artists do the opposite: they **decide the
design on paper first**, where changes are free. The method has five small steps.

1. **Theme and features.** Write the theme in two or three words ("Ocean queen"). Then list **3-5 key
   features**: the things everyone connects with it. Ocean: waves, shells, scales, pearls. Fewer is
   better; the face is small.
2. **Simplify to shapes.** Turn each feature into a shape you can already paint: a wave becomes one
   thick curl ([lesson 02.3](../module-02/lesson-03.md)), a shell becomes a fan of teardrops
   ([lesson 02.2](../module-02/lesson-02.md)), scales become rows of U shapes, pearls become white dots
   ([lesson 02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)). If you can't paint it in a few strokes, simplify it more.
3. **Palette.** Pick one palette type from [lesson 03.5](../module-03/lesson-05.md) that fits the
   **mood**: cool analogous colors for calm water, warm colors for fire. Decide the main color (about
   60%), the second (30%) and a small accent (10%), plus black and white.
4. **Three thumbnails.** A **thumbnail** is a tiny, quick sketch, 3-5 minutes, on a small face
   outline. Draw **the same idea three different ways**: for example a crown on the forehead, a
   one-sided design, a mask around the eyes. Don't fall in love with the first idea.
5. **The one-second read.** Look at the thumbnails small and from far away (prop them up across the
   room, or look at a photo on your phone). The best design is the one you **recognize in one second**
   from about three meters. A clear shape beats lots of small details. Then place the winner on the
   face map, mirror it if it is symmetrical ([lesson 03.4](../module-03/lesson-04.md)), and write the
   plan: parts, palette and order of work, just like the festival plan in
   [lesson 07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md).

## Step by step

1. **Theme.** Write two or three words on the `mood-palette` planner. Add three mood words (calm, wild, royal…).
2. **Features.** List 3-5 key features. Next to each, draw the real thing small, then the brush shape it becomes.
3. **Palette.** Pick a palette type from [lesson 03.5](../module-03/lesson-05.md) and fill the main, second and accent circles.
4. **Thumbnails.** On the `idea-thumbnails` sheet, sketch the idea three ways, 3-5 minutes each. Use colored pencils, not paint.
5. **Distance test.** Prop the sheet up about three meters away, or photograph it and look at the photo small. Tick "reads from 3 m" for each.
6. **Choose.** Keep the clearest one. If two are close, pick the one with one big **star zone** ([lesson 07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md)).
7. **Place it.** Draw the winner on the `face-map`: center line first, then mirror it if it is symmetrical. Mark the eye area.
8. **Write the plan** with the [Design plan](../../templates/design-plan.md): parts, palette, order of work, products.

## Practice exercise

**Three themes, nine thumbnails** (30 minutes).

1. Pick three themes from this list, or your own: ocean, autumn, galaxy, candy, ice, desert sunset.
2. For each, write 3-5 features and their simple shapes on the planner (about 3 minutes per theme).
3. Draw three thumbnails per theme on the `idea-thumbnails` sheets: one centered on the forehead,
   one one-sided, one around the eyes or cheekbones.
4. Do the distance test on all nine and circle the best of each theme.

Stop when every theme has one thumbnail that a friend (or you, with fresh eyes the next day) can
name in one second.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

Stuck? Change only one thing between thumbnails: the **position** (forehead, one side, cheeks), the
**size** of the star zone, or which feature is the **biggest**. Small changes give very different
designs.

</details>

## Mini-project

**From idea to template.** Take your best thumbnail, place it on the `face-map`, write the design
plan, and paint it on a `face-front` sheet from [labs/common](../../labs/common/) in a sheet
protector, in the order from your plan (about 20 minutes).

You're done when:

- [ ] Your planner shows a theme, 3-5 features with their simple shapes, and a palette.
- [ ] You drew at least three thumbnails and chose one with the distance test.
- [ ] The design sits on the center line (or clearly to one side, on purpose), with the eye area marked.
- [ ] Your plan lists parts, palette and order of work, and the painted sheet follows it.

## Common beginner mistakes

| What happens | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The design looks like a pile of stickers | Too many small motifs, no star zone | One big shape for the main feature; drop features until 3-5 remain |
| A feature is too hard to paint | It was copied from a photo, not simplified | Redraw it with only curls, teardrops, dots, lines and U shapes |
| Only one idea, and it doesn't work | Skipped the thumbnails | Draw three versions before choosing; change position or size |
| Colors look muddy or random | No palette chosen | Pick one palette type from 03.5 and stick to 60-30-10 |
| Design ends up lopsided on the face | Not placed on the face map | Start from the center line and landmarks; mirror the half |

## Optional challenge

Make a **theme card set**: six themes, each with features, shapes and palette on one planner page,
and one thumbnail each. Keep them in a folder. When you want to paint, pull a card and you have a
ready plan. Add a seventh card for an idea of your own invention.
