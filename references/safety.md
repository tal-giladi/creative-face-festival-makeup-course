# Safety guide

Face painting is safe and fun when the products are made for skin and you follow a few simple rules.
This page collects them in one place. The lessons explain each one where it comes up. Checked
against current official guidance in October 2026; the sources are listed at the end.

> [!CAUTION]
> Stop and wash the paint off straight away if skin stings, burns, itches, swells or turns red. If
> anything gets into an eye and it still hurts, waters a lot or vision changes after rinsing, get
> medical help.

## The five rules

1. **Only products made for skin.** Face and body paint, cosmetic glitter, cosmetic adhesives. Never
   craft paint, acrylics, markers, craft glitter, school glue, superglue or "black henna".
2. **Eye area only with eye-safe products.** A color that is allowed on the cheek may not be allowed
   on the eyelid. If the label says "not for use near the eyes", believe it, even if the photo on the
   pack shows it near the eyes.
3. **Patch-test every new product** on your inner arm a day or two before you use it on a face.
4. **Clean hands, clean tools, one sponge per person.** Never share eye makeup.
5. **Never paint over broken or irritated skin**: cuts, sores, sunburn, active eczema or rashes, cold
   sores, or anyone with an eye infection.

## Skin-safe, eye-safe or not for skin?

| Product | Face and body | Around the eyes | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water-activated face paint (cosmetic) | Yes | Only if the label allows it | Most face paints are used around the eyes; check the label |
| Fluorescent / neon / UV face paint | Yes, if the label allows | **No** | No fluorescent colors are allowed near the eyes (US FDA) |
| Glow-in-the-dark face paint | Only occasional use (e.g. Halloween) | **No** | Labeled "Do not use in the area of the eye" |
| Cosmetic glitter | Yes | Not on the eyelid or lash line for beginners | Flakes in the eye can scratch it, especially with contact lenses |
| Craft glitter | **No** | **No** | Not made to cosmetic standards; sharp edges |
| Self-adhesive face gems | Yes | Not on the eyelid | Choking hazard for small children |
| Eyelash glue (latex-free) | Small gems | Lash line only, as labeled | Some contain latex: ask about allergies |
| Spirit gum, prosthetic adhesive | Yes, patch-tested | **No** | Spirit gum and its remover are flammable and not for the eyes |
| Superglue, nail glue, craft or school glue | **No** | **No** | If superglue gets on skin, soak in warm water and roll it off; never pull skin apart |
| Markers, acrylic or craft paint | **No** | **No** | "Non-toxic" means safe for art use, not tested for skin |
| "Black henna" | **No** | **No** | Often contains PPD, which can cause blisters, scars and lifelong allergy |
| Kohl, kajal, surma | **Avoid** | **Avoid** | Often contain lead; illegal in the US |
| Decorative contact lenses without a prescription | — | **No** | Can cause serious eye injury |

## Patch test (lesson 01.3)

1. Wash and dry a small patch on the inside of your forearm or elbow.
2. Paint a coin-sized spot with the new product (or apply the glue or glitter base).
3. Leave it on as long as you would wear the design, then wash it off as usual.
4. Watch the spot for 24-48 hours. Redness, itching, swelling or a rash means: don't use that
   product.

Some brands suggest a short 20-minute test for sensitive skin. A longer test is safer, especially
before painting a child or before a long festival day.

## Hygiene

- Wash your hands before and after painting.
- Use a fresh sponge, or a fresh corner, for each person; wash brushes between people.
- Don't put a used sponge back into a paint cake. Mist or wet the cake and load from it with a clean
  brush, or take paint out with a clean spatula.
- Keep paint out of eyes and mouth. Take everything off before you sleep.
- Ask before painting anyone: allergies? sensitive skin? contact lenses? For a child, ask a parent
  or guardian.

## Removal (lesson 01.5)

- **Water-activated face paint:** warm water and mild soap, then a fragrance-free baby wipe for any
  leftover color. Some bright colors stain lightly for a few hours; it fades.
- **Strong pigments:** micellar water on a cotton pad.
- **Cream or oil-based paint, glitter gel:** a gentle oil-based cleanser first, then wash.
- **Glitter:** dab with sticky tape or a damp cloth first, then wash. Close your eyes and wipe away
  from them.
- **Gems and adhesive:** peel gems gently from the edge; use the adhesive's own remover (never near
  the eyes) or an oil-based cleanser for lash glue.
- **Around the eyes:** an eye-safe makeup remover or a gentle oil cleanser on a cotton pad; press,
  wait, then wipe outward.
- **Never on the face:** rubbing alcohol, acetone, nail polish remover, solvent removers.

## Painting children

- Small designs on the cheek, hand or arm for young children; many brands suggest face paint from
  about age 3.
- Patch-test on the child's arm first and get a parent's OK.
- No glitter near children's eyes; keep loose gems, glitter jars and small parts away from toddlers.
