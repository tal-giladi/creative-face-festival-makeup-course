# Materials and substitutes

You do not need an expensive kit. For every item below you get the same four answers: what it is for,
the ideal item, a cheaper or easier-to-find alternative, and what any substitute must be able to do.
Brand names are only examples of a type; any product of the same type works. Shops change what they
stock and prices change often (checked October 2026), so use the "must be able to" line to judge
anything you find.

> [!WARNING]
> Anything that touches skin must be **sold as a cosmetic or face/body paint**. Craft paint,
> acrylics, markers, craft glitter, school glue, superglue and "black henna" are never substitutes,
> however cheap: they are not made with colors approved for skin, can cause rashes or burns, and
> some are hard to get off. Around the eyes, use only products whose label allows eye-area use. See
> the [safety guide](safety.md).

## Start here: the beginner kit

If you buy only five things, buy these. Everything else in the course can wait or be substituted.

| Item | Why first |
|---|---|
| A small set of water-activated face paints (6-12 colors with white and black) | Every lesson uses them |
| One round brush with a sharp point (size 2-4) | Lines, teardrops, dots, details |
| One flat or filbert brush (about 1-1.5 cm wide) | Filling shapes, petals, broad strokes |
| Two or three makeup sponges | Base colors and gradients |
| Fragrance-free baby wipes or a soft cloth | Fixing mistakes and taking paint off |

Water cups, a palette, paper and a mirror you already have at home.

## Face paint

**What it is for:** the color itself. Almost everything in this course is painted with it.

**Ideal:** water-activated face and body paint in solid "cakes" (pots or pans you wet with a brush or
sponge). Professional ones (for example Diamond FX, Wolfe FX, Mehron Paradise, Kryolan Aquacolor,
TAG, Global Colours) give strong color in one layer, dry quickly and don't crack.

**Cheaper or easier to find:** kids' face paint palettes from toy shops, supermarkets or pharmacies
(for example Snazaroo), or a small "starter" palette from an online marketplace **from a brand that
says it meets cosmetic rules** (EU/UK cosmetics regulation or FDA color rules).

**A substitute must:**
- be sold as face paint or body paint (a cosmetic), with an ingredient list on the pack or the
  seller's page;
- wash off with water and mild soap;
- dry to the touch in a minute or two and not stay sticky;
- give a smooth line on a test strip on your arm without much streaking.

**Never use:** acrylic or craft paint, poster or tempera paint, watercolors for paper, markers,
"non-toxic" school paint (non-toxic means safe for art use as intended, not tested for skin).

**Tip:** cheap kids' paints often look streaky in one layer. Let the first layer dry and add a
second thin layer instead of one thick wet one.

## Brushes

**What they are for:** lines, teardrops, petals, dots and filling small shapes.

**Ideal:** synthetic face-painting brushes:
- a round brush with a sharp point, size 2-4 (lines, teardrops, details);
- a smaller round or "liner", size 0-1 (very thin lines, eyelashes, cracks);
- a flat or filbert brush, about 1-1.5 cm wide (filling, petals, broad strokes);
- optional later: a wide flat "one-stroke" brush, 2-2.5 cm, for split cakes (Module 8).

**Cheaper or easier to find:** synthetic watercolor or acrylic brushes from an art shop or
supermarket craft aisle in the same shapes; makeup brushes for the flat/filbert job (a small
concealer brush works well).

**A substitute must:**
- have soft **synthetic** hair that springs back to a point (round) or a sharp edge (flat) when wet;
- not shed hairs;
- be new, or used only for face paint (never a brush that has been in craft paint or solvents);
- be washable with soap and water.

**Test it:** wet it, flick off the water and look: a round brush should come to one sharp point; a
flat should make a straight, thin edge.

## Sponges

**What they are for:** base color, large areas and soft gradients (blends from one color to another).

**Ideal:** high-density face-painting sponges (round or half-round "petal" sponges). They hold a lot of
paint and give smooth coverage.

**Cheaper or easier to find:** makeup wedge sponges or a beauty-blender style sponge (cut it in half
for a flat side). Packs are cheap in pharmacies and supermarkets.

**A substitute must:**
- be fine-pored and soft (rough or coarse pores leave a dotty texture);
- be new and used only for face paint;
- squeeze almost dry without falling apart.

**Hygiene:** use a fresh sponge (or a fresh piece) for each person and wash them after every use.
Never put a used sponge back into a paint cake that another person will use.

## Water containers

**What they are for:** wetting paint and rinsing brushes.

**Ideal:** two water cups: one for rinsing, one kept clean for wetting paint.

**Cheaper or easier to find:** any two clean glasses or jars.

**Must:** be stable (wide base) and hold clean water you change often. Change the water when it gets
cloudy.

## Mixing palette

**What it is for:** mixing colors and checking how wet your paint is before it touches skin.

**Ideal:** a white plastic or ceramic mixing palette.

**Cheaper or easier to find:** a white plate or saucer, a clean plastic lid, a white bathroom tile.

**Must:** be white (so you see the true color), smooth and washable.

## Spray bottle (optional)

**What it is for:** misting paint cakes so they activate faster, and keeping split cakes moist.

**Ideal:** a small fine-mist spray bottle.

**Cheaper:** skip it; wet the cake with a brush and wait 20 seconds.

**Must:** be new or thoroughly washed, filled with clean water, and never pointed at a face.

## Practice surfaces

**What they are for:** practicing without a model.

**Ideal:** the course's printable practice faces (`labs/common/`) on smooth card or heavy paper, slid
into a **clear plastic sheet protector** so you can paint on the plastic, wipe it clean and repeat.

**Cheaper or easier to find:** printer paper (face paint works on it if you don't over-wet it), the
back of a cereal box, a smooth plastic folder. No printer? Draw an oval face with a pencil; the face
map in lesson 03.4 shows where everything goes.

**Then:** the back of your own hand and your inner forearm, then your face in a mirror.

## Cleaning and removal

**What it is for:** fixing mistakes, taking paint off, cleaning brushes and sponges.

**Ideal:** mild soap and warm water; a gentle cleanser; micellar water and cotton pads for strong
pigments; a gentle oil-based cleanser or eye-safe makeup remover around the eyes.

**Cheaper or easier to find:** fragrance-free baby wipes, a soft washcloth, hand soap or dish soap
for brushes and sponges (rinse very well), paper towels.

**Must:** be gentle on skin. Never use rubbing alcohol, acetone, nail polish remover or solvent
removers on the face, and never near the eyes.

## Mirror and light

**What it is for:** painting your own face, checking symmetry.

**Ideal:** a stand-up mirror at face height in daylight or bright, even light.

**Cheaper:** any mirror propped on a table near a window; your phone's front camera for checking
symmetry (it mirrors you, which is what you want).

## Hair and clothing protection

A hairband or clips to keep hair off the face, and an old T-shirt or towel over your shoulders.

## Light sketching

**What it is for:** marking where a design goes before painting.

**Ideal:** a light-colored face paint line with a fine brush, or a white or light brown eye-safe
cosmetic pencil.

**Must:** be a cosmetic pencil or face paint, never a pen, graphite pencil or marker.

## Level 2 and 3 extras (buy only when you get there)

### Cosmetic glitter

**What it is for:** sparkle on cheekbones, temples, hairline and body.

**Ideal:** glitter **sold as cosmetic glitter**, ideally plant-based (cellulose) "bio" glitter
labeled for cosmetic use, or a ready-made cosmetic glitter gel.

**Cheaper or easier to find:** a cosmetic glitter gel from a pharmacy or beauty shop; cosmetic
glitter in small jars.

**A substitute must:** be labeled for skin/cosmetic use, have fine, soft-edged flakes and come
with (or allow) a skin-safe base. **Never use craft glitter** on skin or near the eyes: it is not
made to cosmetic standards and its sharp edges can scratch the eye. In the EU, loose plastic craft
glitter has been banned from sale since October 2023; see lesson 06.1.

### Glitter base (to make glitter stick)

**Ideal:** a cosmetic glitter glue or glitter gel base.

**Cheaper:** plain, fragrance-free aloe vera gel or a little petroleum jelly (body and cheekbones,
not eyelids).

**Must:** be a skin product, wash off with soap and water, and stay out of the eyes.

### Face gems

**Ideal:** self-adhesive face gems (rhinestones on a sticker sheet) made for skin.

**Cheaper:** loose flat-backed acrylic rhinestones fixed with a skin adhesive (below).

**Must:** have a flat back, smooth edges and be small and light. Keep them away from small children
(choking hazard).

### Skin-safe adhesive

**Ideal:** latex-free eyelash glue for small gems; spirit gum (with its own remover) or a
prosthetic adhesive (for example Pros-Aide) for bigger pieces.

**Cheaper:** latex-free eyelash glue from a pharmacy covers most beginner needs.

**Must:** be sold for use on skin; patch-tested first; latex-free if anyone has a latex allergy.
**Never** superglue, nail glue, craft glue or school glue.

### UV / neon face paint

**What it is for:** designs that glow under black light at parties and festivals.

**Ideal:** UV-reactive face and body paint whose label lists where it may be used.

**Must:** be sold as face/body paint. Fluorescent (neon) colors are **not allowed near the eyes** (US
FDA); read the label and keep them on cheeks, forehead, body. See lesson 06.4.

**Also:** a small UV torch (365 nm "black light") to check your design. Never shine it into anyone's
eyes.

### Makeup to combine with face paint

**What it is for:** finishing a festival look (Module 5).

**Ideal:** a primer, a setting spray, eye-safe eyeliner (pencil or liquid) and mascara you already
use.

**Cheaper:** skip the primer (clean, moisturized-then-dry skin works); a light dusting of
translucent powder instead of setting spray.

**Must:** your own, not shared (never share eye makeup), and in date.

### Stencils

**What they are for:** crisp repeated shapes (scales, lace, stars) in Level 3.

**Ideal:** reusable face-painting stencils.

**Cheaper:** cut your own from a thin plastic folder or clean food packaging with scissors or a
craft knife (adults only); fishnet stockings or lace fabric for scales and lace (new, washed).

**Must:** be thin and flexible enough to lie flat on the skin, washable, and free of sharp edges.
